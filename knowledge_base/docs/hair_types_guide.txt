=== HAIR TYPE CLASSIFICATION ===

Hair type is determined by the shape of the hair follicle. A round follicle produces straight hair. An oval follicle produces wavy hair. A very flat or asymmetric follicle produces curly or coily hair. The four main types are Straight (Type 1), Wavy (Type 2), Curly (Type 3), and Coily (Type 4).

=== STRAIGHT HAIR (TYPE 1) ===

Characteristics: Lies completely flat from root to tip. No natural curl or wave pattern. Reflects light easily, making it appear shiny. Tends to get oily quickly because sebum travels from scalp to ends without interruption.

Subtypes:
Type 1A: Very fine and thin. Little volume. Prone to looking limp.
Type 1B: Medium texture with more body than 1A. Holds styles better.
Type 1C: Coarse and thick. Has some body but still falls straight.

Best haircuts for straight hair: Structured cuts like blunt bobs, crew cuts, undercuts, slick backs, and side parts. Straight hair holds clean lines well.

Products: Volumising mousse for lift. Lightweight serums for shine. Avoid heavy oils which weigh straight hair down.
Styling: Blow dry with a round brush for volume. Flat iron for extra smoothness if desired.
Washing: May need washing every 1-2 days due to oil buildup.
Avoid: Heavy conditioners on the scalp. Silicone-heavy products that build up quickly.

=== WAVY HAIR (TYPE 2) ===

Characteristics: Natural S-shaped wave pattern. Falls between straight and curly. Has more volume than straight hair and more shine than curly hair. The wave pattern can loosen or tighten depending on humidity.

Subtypes:
Type 2A: Loose, fine waves. Easy to straighten or define. Most versatile wave type.
Type 2B: More defined waves, slightly frizzy. Waves begin from the mid-length rather than the root.
Type 2C: Strong waves close to loose curls. Can be thick and prone to frizz.

Best haircuts for wavy hair: Layered cuts, lobs, textured crops, quiffs, and medium length styles that allow the natural wave to show. Layers are especially important to prevent the hair from becoming triangular (wide at the bottom).

Products: Sea salt spray to enhance natural waves. Light curl cream or mousse. Diffuser when blow drying.
Styling: Scrunch product into damp hair and diffuse or air dry. Avoid brushing dry or frizz increases.
Washing: Every 2-3 days is typical. Over-washing strips natural oils that help define waves.
Avoid: Brushing dry hair. Heavy pomades. Silicone serums that coat and weigh waves down.

=== CURLY HAIR (TYPE 3) ===

Characteristics: Defined spiral or ringlet curl pattern. Has significant shrinkage — curly hair appears much shorter dry than wet. Prone to dryness because the curl pattern prevents scalp oils from travelling down the hair shaft. Frizz is common.

Subtypes:
Type 3A: Large loose spirals. Shiny with a clear curl pattern. Less prone to frizz than 3B or 3C.
Type 3B: Medium spiral curls with more volume. More prone to frizz. Springs back when stretched.
Type 3C: Tight corkscrew curls. High density and volume. Maximum shrinkage. Can look much shorter than actual length.

Best haircuts for curly hair: The Deva Cut (dry curl cutting technique) where each curl is cut individually. Layered cuts to remove weight. Undercut or fade at the sides with curly top. The stylist should cut dry or ask for a Deva Cut specialist. Avoid blunt cuts that create a triangular shape.

Products: Curl defining cream. Leave-in conditioner. Gel for hold. Deep conditioning mask weekly.
Styling: Apply products to soaking wet hair. Scrunch upward. Diffuse on low heat or air dry. Avoid touching while drying.
The Curly Girl Method: Avoid sulphates (harsh shampoos) and silicones. Co-wash (conditioner only wash) is popular for type 3 hair.
Washing: 1-3 times per week. Over-washing strips moisture that curls need to stay defined.
Avoid: Brushing or combing dry curly hair. Rough towel drying. High heat without heat protectant.

=== COILY HAIR (TYPE 4) ===

Characteristics: Very tight coil or zig-zag curl pattern. The most fragile hair type due to its shape. Extremely high shrinkage — can shrink up to 75% of actual length. Appears dry because oils cannot travel down the coiled shaft. Densely packed but each individual strand is fine. Requires maximum moisture.

Subtypes:
Type 4A: Soft coils with an S pattern. Defined and springy when moisturised.
Type 4B: Z-pattern (zig-zag) coils. Less defined than 4A but very dense. Bends sharply rather than spiralling.
Type 4C: Very tight zig-zag coils, often not visibly defined without product. Maximum shrinkage. Very fragile and prone to breakage.

Best haircuts for coily hair: The TWA (Teeny Weeny Afro), tapered cut, the afro, protective styles (braids, locs, twists), wash-and-go.

Products: LOC Method (Liquid, Oil, Cream) — apply water-based leave-in first, then an oil to seal, then a cream for definition. Shea butter, castor oil, and coconut oil are popular. Avoid sulphates and silicones.
Styling: Stretch styles (banding, twist-outs, braid-outs) show length without damage. Protective styles protect ends and retain length.
Washing: Once per week or less. Always follow with deep conditioning. Co-washing between wash days.
Avoid: Fine-tooth combs on dry hair. Frequent heat styling. Tight styles that pull on the scalp and cause traction alopecia.

=== HAIR TEXTURE ===

Hair texture refers to the thickness of each individual strand and is separate from hair type.

FINE TEXTURE
Individual strands are thin. Hair can be straight, wavy, or curly and still be fine in texture.
Characteristics: Lightweight. Prone to looking flat. Lacks volume naturally. Shows oiliness quickly.
Best styles: Shorter to medium cuts. Blunt ends to create the illusion of thickness. Avoid very long heavy styles.
Products: Volumising mousse and lightweight sprays. Avoid heavy waxes, butters, or oils at the root.

MEDIUM TEXTURE
The most common texture. Moderate thickness per strand.
Characteristics: Holds styles well. Has natural body. Works with a wide range of products.
Best styles: Most styles work well. Focus on face shape guidance as the primary factor.

THICK TEXTURE
Individual strands are wide in diameter. Often dense as well (many strands per square centimetre).
Characteristics: Heavy. Resistant to styling. Prone to frizz and bulk. Holds styles very well once set.
Best styles: Cuts with thinning or layering to reduce bulk. The barber or stylist should use thinning shears.
Products: Smoothing creams and serums to control frizz and weight. Medium to strong hold products.
Avoid: Very blunt cuts on all lengths that make thick hair look like a helmet. Skipping conditioner.
